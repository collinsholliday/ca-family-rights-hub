01 · Child support

Understand the order before you act.

A support order, an agency case, and the payment history are related but not identical. First identify who issued or manages the order and whether a Local Child Support Agency (LCSA) is involved.

  • Get a complete copy of the current order and any later modifications.
  • Request or download a payment history from the agency or court record.
  • Keep proof of direct payments, credits, and disputed amounts.
  • Do not assume a private agreement changes a court order.
  • Ask the LCSA or a lawyer how to address arrears, interest, or enforcement in your case.
California Courts: child support guide ↗
A useful first question

Is the Local Child Support Agency part of the case?

California Courts explains that the process to ask for or change support can differ when an LCSA is involved. If you are unsure, the court's guide lists the Child Support Customer Connect line.

Customer Connect: 866-901-3212

Verify current contact details and case-specific procedures with the agency before relying on them.

02 · Dedicated guide

Inheritance & Unpaid Child Support

When a parent who owes child support dies, families may have questions about unpaid support, an estate, inheritance, and who is allowed to act. This is a specialized intersection of family law and probate—not an automatic claim to a beneficiary's inheritance.

Start with the records

Collect the support order, case number, official payment history, notices, and any correspondence about arrears. Keep certified copies where possible.

Identify the estate process

Find out whether a probate case has been opened, who the personal representative is, and whether a claims deadline applies. Do not contact heirs as though they personally owe the debt without legal advice.

Ask who can make a claim

Whether arrears can be asserted against an estate, who has standing, what amount is legally due, and how a claim is prioritized depend on the order, assignment status, probate rules, and case facts.

Important distinction

Inheritance is not automatically child support.

A child-support arrears balance does not by itself establish that a child or other beneficiary can take money directly from an estate. A probate attorney and the relevant child-support agency can assess the correct claimant, procedure, and deadlines.

Before signing a release, accepting a distribution, or missing a probate deadline, get case-specific advice.

Practical document checklist

Records and official PDFs — in collection order

Start by gathering records; then use only the court forms that fit the estate and your legal role. This is an educational roadmap, not a mandatory packet or a promise that child-support arrears can be taken from an estate.

First, request these records from the agency

  1. Current and prior child-support orders, including modifications.
  2. A complete official payment history and arrears balance as of the date of death, with interest separately identified.
  3. A breakdown of assigned versus non-assigned arrears and any state-assistance assignment.
  4. Written confirmation of case status, the agency's position on continued enforcement after the parent's death, and the person legally authorized to receive case information.

California case records are confidential. Customer Connect access requires a participant ID and PIN; a relative or beneficiary should not assume they can access another person's account. The agency may require proof of authority from the estate's personal representative.

  1. Customer Connect factsheet (PDF). Use it to understand how an authorized case participant can access case information and print payment history. Open official PDF ↗
  2. DCSS Declaration of Support Payment History (DCSS 0569) (PDF, included in the application packet). Use only if the agency asks for a declaration or supporting payment-history schedule; it is not a substitute for the agency's certified ledger. Open official packet PDF ↗
  3. Death certificate, will/codicils, trust papers, and proof of relationship. These are records to obtain from the appropriate custodian; they are not one universal Judicial Council form. Request certified copies when required.
  4. DE-111 — Petition for Probate (PDF). Relevant only if a probate proceeding needs to be opened and the person filing is legally entitled to petition. Download official PDF ↗
  5. DE-147 — Duties and Liabilities of Personal Representative. For the person appointed to administer the estate; not a general inheritance application. Official form and PDF ↗
  6. DE-150 — Letters. Court-issued proof of the personal representative's authority after appointment; obtain the issued/certified copy from the probate court. Official form information ↗
  7. DE-154 — Request for Special Notice. May help an eligible interested person request notice of specified probate filings; check eligibility and local filing instructions. Official form and PDF ↗
  8. DE-157 — Notice of Administration to Creditors. Usually prepared/served as part of estate administration; it alerts creditors to claim procedures and deadlines. Official form and PDF ↗
  9. DE-160 / DE-161 — Inventory and Appraisal / Attachment. Estate inventory documents generally handled by the personal representative and probate referee, not an heir's claim form. Official forms and PDFs ↗
  10. DE-172 — Creditor's Claim (PDF). Do not file this automatically. A California probate lawyer must first assess whether the child-support obligation is a valid claim against this estate, who has standing to assert it, and whether the claim deadline applies. Download official PDF ↗
  11. DE-174 — Allowance or Rejection of Creditor's Claim. This is generally for the personal representative to respond to a filed creditor claim; it is not the beneficiary's claim form. Official form and PDF ↗
Important legal distinction: California DCSS's public materials do not establish a universal process allowing a child or beneficiary to collect a deceased parent's child-support arrears directly as an inheritance. The correct claimant, whether the obligation is enforceable against the estate, the effect of assigned arrears, priority, and deadlines require review of the order, payment ledger, assistance history, probate file, and current law by a qualified California probate/family-law attorney and the responsible LCSA. Never assume a beneficiary personally owes the decedent's child-support debt.

Official starting points: California DCSS Customer Connect · DCSS records guidance · California Courts probate guide.

This section is educational only. It does not determine whether a debt survives death, whether an estate claim is valid, or whether a particular distribution can be reached. Those questions require review of the order, payment ledger, probate file, and applicable law.
03 · Custody & parenting time

Keep the focus on the child and the order.

California Courts provides step-by-step guides for requesting or changing custody and parenting-time orders. Existing orders, case history, and local court rules matter.

  • Use the current court order as your starting point.
  • Keep a neutral calendar of exchanges, missed visits, and important events.
  • Save relevant messages in their original form and note dates.
  • Separate firsthand facts from assumptions or secondhand reports.
  • Ask the court self-help center about local procedures and forms.
California Courts: custody guide ↗

Need to change an order?

California Courts describes using a Request for Order (FL-300) for many requests. Attachments and supporting declarations may be needed depending on what you are asking the judge to decide.

04 · Practical toolkit

Build a court-prep packet.

A simple organizer to help you prepare for a self-help appointment or attorney consultation. It does not submit anything to a court or store your information on this website.

Document checklist

  • Current and prior court orders
  • Case number and court location
  • Official child-support payment history
  • Relevant notices and filed documents
  • Income and expense records, if relevant
  • Chronology of important events
  • Copies of relevant messages or receipts
  • Questions and desired outcomes
